# Week 16 — Assess, explain and publish your portfolio

**Prerequisites:** Completed earlier checkpoints or an honest list of gaps.

**Time:** six 60-minute sessions; Day 7 is rest or catch-up. For each session, read for about 15 minutes, practice for 35 and write evidence for 10. Split a difficult session over two days if necessary.

## Understand

An assessment connects a claim to evidence and a limitation. Your final portfolio should let someone reproduce a small demonstration and understand where the demonstration stops.

## Do these sessions in order

1. **Day 1.** Read [the capstone brief](../05-portfolio-projects/CAPSTONE.md). Choose evidence from your HTTP notes, lab reports, protected app and mock-finance work.

2. **Day 2.** Re-run the local Django tests and the two financial simulations. Record versions, commands, dates and actual outputs. A failed result stays visible until fixed.

3. **Day 3.** Review the final test matrix against your threat model. Mark every control tested, partially tested, planned or not applicable with a reason.

4. **Day 4.** Write the assessment using the report template. Include scope, architecture, three findings, fixes, evidence and residual risk.

5. **Day 5.** Review every file and image for secrets and private data. Check navigation from README to weekly lessons, reports and project evidence. Use the upload guide if publishing manually.

6. **Day 6.** Give a five-minute explanation: one request, one vulnerability and defense, one ownership test, one duplicate-transfer test, and one important limitation. Choose your next learning goal from Module 5.

## Evidence

Save `progress/final-assessment.md`, update the tracker and link only completed artifacts from your portfolio index.

## Checkpoint

Meet the capstone acceptance criteria and explain every unsupported claim. Completion is a foundation, not a claim of professional banking qualification.

Reading: [ASVS](https://owasp.org/projects/asvs) for scoping a small set of verification requirements.
